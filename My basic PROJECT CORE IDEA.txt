My basic PROJECT CORE IDEA
Your project is about WiFi congestion in crowded university areas.
The problem is not only “slow internet”.
The real engineering problem is this:
Too many students connect to one WiFi access point, while other nearby access points are free or less busy.
This happens because most devices choose WiFi based on signal strength, not based on how busy the access point is.
WHAT IS AN ACCESS POINT?
An access point, AP, is the WiFi device that your laptop or phone connects to.
People call it a router, but in campus networks it is usually called an access point.
Example:
AP 1 has strong signal and 80 students connected.
AP 2 has slightly weaker signal and only 10 students connected.
Your laptop may still connect to AP 1 because the signal is stronger.
That causes congestion.
WHAT IS CONGESTION?
Congestion means too many devices are trying to use the same limited network resource at the same time.
Like:
One AP has limited bandwidth.
If 5 students share it, each student gets good speed.
If 80 students share it, each student gets slow speed.
WHY YOUR SUPERVISOR WAS CONCERNED
Your first idea was too big.
It wanted to:
Force devices to change APs.
Use LSTM AI.
Use federated learning.
Use 802.11r roaming.
Control WiFi behavior automatically.
That is too difficult for a final-year project because:
You may not have access to university AP settings.
Operating systems do not easily allow apps to force WiFi roaming.
AI training needs a lot of data.
Federated learning is advanced.
802.11r depends on campus network configuration.
So the project must become simpler and buildable.
FINAL WORKING SOLUTION
Your final solution should be:
Passive Intelligent WiFi Monitoring and Recommendation System
Meaning:
The system will not force users to move.
It will monitor nearby WiFi access points.
It will calculate which AP is better.
It will recommend the best AP to the user or IT admin.
It will show congestion status on a dashboard.
This is feasible.

Step 1: Scan nearby WiFi networks.

Step 3: Calculate a quality score for each AP.
Step 4: Compare APs.
Step 5: Detect overloaded APs.
Step 6: Recommend the best AP.

STEP 1: DEVICE SCANS WIFI
You use a laptop or Raspberry Pi to scan WiFi.
It looks for all nearby access points.
It collects:
SSID
BSSID
RSSI
Channel
Frequency
Signal quality
WHAT IS SSID?
SSID is the WiFi name.
Example:
UJ-WiFi
Eduroam
WHAT IS BSSID?
BSSID is the unique address of one AP.
Many APs can use the same WiFi name.
Example:
SSID: Eduroam
AP 1 BSSID: AA:11:22:33:44:55
AP 2 BSSID: BB:11:22:33:44:55
So BSSID helps you identify the exact AP.
WHAT IS RSSI?
RSSI means Received Signal Strength Indicator.
It shows how strong the WiFi signal is.
It is measured in dBm.
Example:
-40 dBm means very strong.
-60 dBm means good.
-75 dBm means weak.
-90 dBm means very poor.
STEP 2: COLLECT NETWORK PERFORMANCE DATA
Signal strength alone is not enough.
You also need performance data.
Your system should measure:
Ping delay
Packet loss
Download speed or estimated throughput
Number of visible APs
Current connected AP
WHAT IS PING?
Ping measures delay.
It sends a small message to a server and waits for a reply.
Example:
Ping = 10 ms means good.
Ping = 100 ms means slow.
Ping = 300 ms means bad.
WHAT IS PACKET LOSS?
Packet loss means some data does not arrive.
Example:
If you send 100 packets and 5 fail, packet loss is 5%.
High packet loss means unstable connection.
WHAT IS THROUGHPUT?
Throughput means actual data speed.
Example:
20 Mbps means the connection can move about 20 megabits per second.
STEP 3: CREATE A QUALITY SCORE
The system gives every AP a score.
Example:
AP Score = Signal Score + Latency Score + Packet Loss Score + Load Estimate Score
Simple meaning:
Strong signal gives higher score.
Low delay gives higher score.
Low packet loss gives higher score.
Less congestion gives higher score.
Example:
AP 1:
RSSI = -45 dBm, strong
Ping = 180 ms, bad
Packet loss = 8%, bad
Score = 55%
AP 2:
RSSI = -60 dBm, good
Ping = 25 ms, good
Packet loss = 1%, good
Score = 85%
Even though AP 1 has stronger signal, AP 2 is better.
This is the heart of your project.
STEP 4: COMPARE ACCESS POINTS
The system compares all nearby APs.
It asks:
Which AP has the best score?
Is the current AP congested?
Is another AP better?
Is the better AP much better, or only slightly better?
You need a threshold.
WHAT IS A THRESHOLD?
A threshold is a limit used to make a decision.
Example:
Only recommend switching if another AP is at least 20% better.
This prevents unnecessary switching.
